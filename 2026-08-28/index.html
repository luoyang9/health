01 / WEEK AT A GLANCE

What to keep—and what to watch

This is an observational review of a short window, not a score or a diagnosis.

Working well

  • Strength consistency: six strength sessions were recorded during the report window.
  • Movement variety: strength, walking, and table tennis created a balanced week.
  • Sleep quantity: the seven-night average reached 7.55 hours.
  • Stable recovery: resting heart rate stayed in a narrow 60–66 bpm range.

Worth watching

  • Sleep variability: duration ranged from 5.63 to 8.36 hours.
  • Timing variability: the median sleep start was about 1:39 a.m., with one start at 3:26 a.m.
  • Fragmentation: Aug 25 had 79.1% efficiency and nearly 50 minutes classified awake.
  • Fiber logging: entries varied widely; confirm whether the low day was intake or incomplete logging.

08 / METHODS & NOTES

How to interpret this snapshot

Coverage and source differences affect which comparisons are meaningful.

Coverage and duplicate sleep sources
  • Daily averages use Aug 20–25, the six complete metric days. Aug 19 and Aug 26 were partial and excluded.
  • Seven primary sleep sessions use the overlapping record that included time-in-bed data. Exact awakening counts remain uncertain because the two synced sleep sources disagree.
  • VO₂ max has only two nearly identical samples: 43.47 and 43.62. That is an initial reference, not a direction.
Medical boundary
Wearable data supports pattern recognition but cannot rule conditions in or out. Persistent low-efficiency sleep, loud snoring, gasping, morning headaches, excessive daytime sleepiness, sustained heart-rate changes, or concerning symptoms belong with a qualified clinician.

Training safety note

Use controlled technique, breathe through effort, and avoid prolonged breath-holding. If you have an eye condition or receive activity-specific guidance from a clinician, that guidance overrides this report.
